Clackamas housing: a little of everything

Clackamas grew up around the Sunnyside Road and Highway 212 corridors, and its homes reflect several decades of building. You will find 1970s and 1980s ranches and split-levels, 1990s and 2000s two-story homes, and a large number of townhomes, condos and apartment communities near the Town Center and the MAX Green Line.

That mix means very different flooring projects: a whole-house replacement in a single-family home, a quick turnover in a rental, or a condo where the HOA sets the rules for underlayment and work hours. We start by understanding which of those your project is.

Condos, townhomes and shared walls

In attached homes, sound travels. Many HOAs require hard floors to be installed over an underlayment with a minimum sound rating, and some require approval before work begins. Read your association's rules before buying material; the right underlayment is part of the material choice, not an afterthought.

Townhomes are often multi-level with stairs between every floor, so stair flooring and landings usually belong in the plan. Condo buildings can also have rules about elevators, parking and where old flooring can be disposed of.

Account for the whole installation

A material price is only one part of a flooring budget. In Clackamas homes built across several decades, the other parts vary widely:

  • Removal: carpet comes up quickly; glued vinyl or tile takes longer (removal)
  • Subfloor: older homes may need squeak repairs or leveling; newer homes usually need less (subfloor preparation)
  • Edges: baseboards, shoe molding and transitions at every doorway (baseboards and transitions)
  • Moving and access: furniture, appliances, stairs and parking

Our installation cost guide walks through each of these so a quote is easy to compare.

Moving furniture and keeping your home usable

Many Clackamas projects are in occupied homes and apartments where there is not much space to move furniture. Before installation, it helps to decide which rooms will be done first, where furniture and boxes can go, and which items need help moving. Appliances such as refrigerators, ranges and washers may need to be disconnected and reset.

In condos and apartments, plan for parking, elevator access and where old flooring and packaging will be removed. Share those details when you request a quote, and we will reflect them in the scope and schedule.

Moisture and the flooring you choose

Like the rest of the Portland area, Clackamas has damp winters. Homes built on slabs or over crawlspaces benefit from a moisture check before wood or glue-down floors are installed. Rigid-core LVP and tile are forgiving choices for kitchens, baths, laundry rooms and ground-floor condos; engineered wood gives a real wood look with better stability than solid boards; and carpet remains a comfortable choice for bedrooms and upper floors.

Rental owners near the Town Center can find product and scheduling advice on our rental flooring page.

Questions

Clackamas flooring questions

Do you install flooring in Clackamas condos and townhomes?

Yes. Share your HOA's flooring and underlayment requirements and any rules about work hours or disposal so they are included in the scope.

What underlayment does my condo need?

Your HOA decides. Many require an underlayment with a minimum sound rating for hard floors. Check the rules before buying material.

What does a flooring quote include besides material?

Usually removal, subfloor preparation, installation labor, trim and transitions, and any stairs. The material price guide covers material only.

Can you work on rental units in Clackamas?

Yes. Tell us vacancy and move-in dates, access details and the rooms involved, and we will confirm timing after reviewing the scope.

Vancouver homes, old and new

Close to downtown, neighborhoods such as Hough, Arnada, Shumway and Carter Park include early-1900s homes and bungalows, some with original fir floors and the same quirks as older Portland houses: uneven floors, old trim and layered materials. Original fir that is still sound can often be sanded and refinished (hardwood floor refinishing). Uptown Village and the downtown waterfront add newer condos and apartments.

East Vancouver, around Cascade Park, Fisher's Landing and Mill Plain, grew rapidly in the 1980s through the 2000s, with two-story homes, family rooms and carpeted stairs. North of the city, unincorporated communities like Hazel Dell, Salmon Creek and Felida have a similar mix of mid-century ranches and newer subdivisions.

Coordinate the estimate and material order

  • Delivery and staging: confirm where material will be delivered and stored so it can acclimate as the manufacturer requires
  • Timing: share your preferred dates; we confirm the schedule after reviewing the scope and material availability

Columbia River weather and your floors

Vancouver shares the Portland area's wet fall-to-spring season, and east Vancouver near the Columbia River Gorge gets the cold, dry east winds that blow through in winter. Those swings in humidity affect wood floors, so products with good dimensional stability, like engineered wood and rigid-core LVP, are popular. Homes over crawlspaces benefit from a subfloor moisture check before wood is installed, and basements and slab rooms are good candidates for waterproof flooring.

Two-story homes, stairs and open main floors

Many east Vancouver homes have two stories with a carpeted stair, a family room and an open kitchen on the main floor. Replacing the main-floor carpet and vinyl with one continuous hard floor is a common upgrade, often paired with new stair carpet or LVP with matching stair nosing.

Long, open runs may need a transition in a doorway, and kitchen islands and cabinets affect how a floating floor is installed. We map the layout, the stair details and the transitions before installation, so the finished floor looks intentional throughout the house.

Flooring choices for Vancouver homes

  • LVP for kitchens, baths, entries, rentals and basements
  • Engineered or solid hardwood for main living areas (solid hardwood)
  • Laminate for bedrooms, offices and budget-conscious projects (laminate)
  • Tile for bathrooms, laundry rooms and mudrooms (tile)
  • Carpet for bedrooms, family rooms and stairs (carpet)

Two-story homes often need stair flooring to match a new main floor. See our installation cost guide for everything a quote typically covers.

Vancouver flooring questions

Do you install flooring in Vancouver, WA?

Yes. Vancouver is part of our service area. Call or text (503) 421-1730 or send your project details for a free preliminary estimate.

Does the Price Guide include Washington sales tax?

No. The Price Guide shows material-only planning ranges and excludes taxes, delivery and installation. Factor in tax based on where material is purchased and delivered.

What flooring holds up best to Vancouver's winters?

Dimensionally stable floors such as engineered wood and rigid-core LVP handle humidity swings well. A subfloor moisture check before installation is important for wood.

Can you install flooring in older homes near downtown Vancouver?

Yes. Older homes need extra planning for uneven subfloors, original trim and older materials. Tell us the age of the home when requesting an estimate.

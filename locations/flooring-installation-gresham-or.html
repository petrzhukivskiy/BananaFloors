Gresham homes we see most often

Gresham is one of Oregon's largest cities, and much of its housing was built in the second half of the 20th century. Single-level ranches, split-levels and two-story homes from the 1960s through the 1990s make up a large share of neighborhoods like Rockwood, Centennial and Powell Valley. Many of these houses still have their original carpet in living areas and bedrooms, with sheet vinyl or older laminate in kitchens and baths.

Newer subdivisions toward Pleasant Valley and the southeast edge of the city were built more recently and often have open main floors where a single plank floor can run through the living room, dining area and kitchen. Gresham also has many apartments, duplexes and rental homes, where durability and easy repairs matter more than anything else.

Split-levels, short stair runs and transitions

Split-level houses are a Gresham staple, and they bring a specific planning challenge: short flights of stairs between levels, plus several doorways where carpet, vinyl and wood may all meet. When you replace one level, think about how the stair flooring and landings will match, and where each transition will sit (baseboards and transitions).

Older ranch homes often have flooring layers stacked over the years. Removing the old layers (removal) usually gives a better result than covering them, and lets us check the subfloor for squeaks or soft spots before installation.

East winds, wet winters and your floors

Gresham shares the Portland area's wet fall-to-spring season, and it is also close enough to the Columbia River Gorge to feel the cold, dry east winds that blow through in winter. Those dry spells, combined with indoor heating, can open small gaps in wood floors; damp seasons do the opposite. Products with good dimensional stability, such as engineered wood and rigid-core LVP, handle those swings well.

For homes over crawlspaces, the vapor barrier underneath matters. We check subfloor moisture before installing wood and let you know if something below the floor should be addressed first.

Rentals, duplexes and multi-unit homes in Gresham

Gresham has a large share of rental housing, from single-family rentals to duplexes and apartment communities. For those properties, the goal is a floor that survives several tenancies and can be repaired without replacing a whole room. Rigid-core LVP with a thicker wear layer, durable stain-resistant carpet in bedrooms, and a consistent product across units are common choices.

When you request an estimate for a rental, include the vacancy dates, the number of units and rooms, access instructions and whether other trades are scheduled. Turnover timing is usually the tightest part of the project, so the earlier we see the scope, the easier it is to plan.

Flooring choices that suit Gresham homes

  • LVP for kitchens, baths, entries and rentals, where water and pets are part of daily life
  • Laminate for bedrooms and living areas on a tighter budget (laminate)
  • Carpet for bedrooms, family rooms and split-level stairs (carpet)
  • Hardwood or engineered wood for main living areas where you want real wood
  • Tile for bathrooms, laundry rooms and mudrooms (tile)

Gresham flooring questions

Do you install flooring in Gresham?

Yes. Gresham is part of our regular service area. Send your project details or call or text (503) 421-1730 to request a free preliminary estimate.

What flooring is good for a split-level home?

Many split-level homeowners use one hard floor such as LVP across the main level and carpet or matching stair nosing on the short stair runs. Planning transitions between levels is the key.

Can you remove old carpet and vinyl before installing new floors?

Removal can be part of your scope. Tell us what is on the floor now; older vinyl in homes built before 2004 may need testing before it is disturbed.

Is LVP a good choice for Gresham rentals?

LVP is a popular rental floor because the planks resist water and scratches and damaged planks can often be replaced. Choose a thicker wear layer for high traffic.

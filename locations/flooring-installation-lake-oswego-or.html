Wood floors and the details that make them work

Wood is a common choice in Lake Oswego, and the details decide whether a new wood floor looks like it belongs. Board width and species should suit the house: narrower oak strip is typical of older homes, while wider planks in white oak are popular in remodeled and newer homes. Prefinished boards install with less disruption; site-finished floors allow custom stain and a flush surface but need drying time.

Where a new floor meets an existing one, the transition should be deliberate: a flush seam, a threshold, or a change in direction at a doorway. We plan those locations, along with baseboards, shoe molding and door clearances, before installation begins (baseboards and transitions).

Homes across Lake Oswego

Neighborhoods such as First Addition, Evergreen and areas around Old Town include early-20th-century cottages and English-style homes, often with original oak or fir floors and character trim worth preserving. If the existing wood is sound, hardwood floor refinishing is often the first option to consider. Mountain Park, Palisades and Forest Highlands have many homes from the 1960s through the 1980s, often on hillsides with split floor plans and daylight lower levels. Westlake, Lake Grove and the Lakewood area add a range of styles, including larger homes from more recent decades.

That variety means very different subfloors: old planks with gaps and nails, plywood over joists, and concrete in lower levels. We check what is actually under your floor before recommending a product (subfloor preparation).

Hillsides, lower levels and moisture

Hillside lots are common in Lake Oswego, and many homes have daylight basements or lower levels partly below grade. Solid hardwood is generally not recommended on below-grade concrete; engineered wood (glued or floated over the right underlayment), rigid-core LVP and tile are better fits. Concrete moisture should be checked before anything is installed.

Homes near the lake, creeks and wooded slopes can also have damp crawlspaces. A moisture check of the wood subfloor before installing solid hardwood helps avoid cupping later.

Carpet, tile and the rest of the house

Wood gets most of the attention, but Lake Oswego homes need other floors too. Bedrooms and media rooms are often carpeted for comfort and quiet, with pad matched to the carpet's warranty requirements. Bathrooms, laundry rooms and mudrooms do best with porcelain or ceramic tile over the right underlayment or membrane.

Where several materials meet, such as a tiled entry next to a wood hallway and a carpeted stair, the heights and transitions need to be planned together. We include those edges in the scope so the finished floors read as one project rather than separate rooms.

Planning a Lake Oswego flooring project

  • Choose a wood species, width and finish that suit the house, and order samples to see them in your light
  • Plan transitions to any floors that are staying, and decide on baseboards
  • Check lower-level slabs and crawlspaces for moisture
  • Include stairs in the plan if the flooring continues up or down (stair flooring)

Lake Oswego flooring questions

What wood flooring works best in Lake Oswego homes?

Oak remains the most common choice. Narrow strip suits many older homes; wider white oak planks are popular in remodels. Engineered wood is a strong option for lower levels and over concrete.

Can new hardwood blend with my existing floors?

A perfect match is difficult, but compatible species, width and finish, plus a well-placed transition, can blend new and existing floors. Send photos for a review.

Is solid hardwood OK in a daylight basement?

Solid hardwood is generally not recommended below grade or directly on concrete. Engineered wood, LVP or tile are better choices there.

Do you install flooring in Mountain Park and Westlake?

Yes. Both are part of our Lake Oswego service area.

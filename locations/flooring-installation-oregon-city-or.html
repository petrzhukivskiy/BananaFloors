Historic homes in McLoughlin and Canemah

Oregon City was incorporated in 1844, and its historic neighborhoods show it. The McLoughlin neighborhood on the bluff above downtown and the Canemah historic district to the south have homes from the late 1800s and early 1900s, many with original fir floors, tall painted baseboards and framing that has settled over a century or more.

In these homes, flooring decisions are as much about preservation as about style. Floors may slope, doorways may not be square, and original trim is often worth keeping. Before choosing a product, we look at how much the floor deviates, whether it should be flattened (subfloor preparation), and how a new floor will meet original wood in adjacent rooms (baseboards and transitions).

Mid-century homes and newer neighborhoods

Beyond the historic core, Oregon City has mid-century ranches and a wide band of newer subdivisions toward Park Place, the South End and Beavercreek Road. Those homes look more like the rest of the metro: carpet and vinyl that are ready for replacement, open kitchens, and two-story layouts with carpeted stairs.

For these houses, LVP is a common choice for main floors and kitchens, carpet for bedrooms, and engineered wood where owners want real wood with good stability.

Coordinate flooring with your remodel

Flooring is often one part of a bigger project in Oregon City homes: a kitchen update, a bathroom remodel, or a whole-house refresh before a sale. The order of work matters. Many floating floors should not be installed under cabinets, tile floors may need to go in before a new vanity or toilet, and painting is usually easier before baseboards go back on.

  • Tell us which trades are involved and the order you are planning
  • Decide whether cabinets, islands or vanities go in before or after flooring
  • Plan floor heights at doorways, dishwashers and exterior doors
  • Leave time for removal and preparation before installation (removal)

Floors on the bluff and in the hills

Oregon City's geography, with downtown along the river and neighborhoods on the bluff and the hills above, means a mix of foundations. Older homes may have low crawlspaces with limited access, while newer hillside homes can have lower levels on concrete. Each affects which floor is a good fit.

For a crawlspace home, we check the wood subfloor for moisture and soft spots before installing anything. For lower-level slabs, rigid-core LVP, tile or engineered wood approved for concrete are common choices, with a vapor barrier if the manufacturer requires one. The floor you see is only as good as what is under it.

Older materials, moisture and the river

Many Oregon City homes predate modern materials rules. If old sheet vinyl, vinyl tile or painted trim will be disturbed, tell us when the house was built so any testing happens before work starts. Homes near the Willamette and Clackamas rivers and on hillside lots can also have damp crawlspaces or lower levels; we check subfloor moisture before installing wood and recommend waterproof flooring options for basements and wet rooms.

Questions

Oregon City flooring questions

Can you install new flooring in a historic Oregon City home?

Yes. Older homes need extra planning for uneven floors, original trim and transitions to original wood. Tell us the age of the home and send photos of the rooms.

Should flooring go in before or after new kitchen cabinets?

It depends on the product. Many floating floors should not be installed under cabinets or islands, while some glue-down and tile floors can be. We match the sequence to the product requirements.

My floors slope. Can they be leveled?

Some slope is normal in older homes. Flooring needs a flat surface more than a perfectly level one; we check flatness and recommend preparation that fits the product and the house.

Do I need to test old vinyl before removal?

If your home was built before 2004 and has old sheet vinyl, vinyl tile or adhesive, testing for asbestos is generally required before it is disturbed. Licensed specialists handle testing and any abatement.

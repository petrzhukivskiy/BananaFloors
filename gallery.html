Flooring styles

Style visualizations, not job photos

Every image on this page is a style visualization. It is a typical room put together so you can compare color, grain, plank width and how a floor sits in a space. These are not photographs of finished Banana Floors jobs. Colors and finishes vary by brand, dye lot and the light in the room. Use the pictures to narrow a direction, then open the installation page for how that floor is actually put in.

Match a look to a floor we install

Solid hardwood is the living-room look: wide planks, visible grain and a finish meant to stay. In older Portland bungalows an original fir or oak floor is sometimes worth refinishing instead of covering. Engineered wood, shown in the dining scene, has a real wood surface on a layered core, so it stays more stable when indoor humidity swings between a wet winter and a dry heated summer.

Luxury vinyl plank is the kitchen visualization, a wood look that handles spills and a basement slab. We install click-lock and glue-down LVP and talk through wear layer and thickness before you buy. Laminate, in the office scene, is the crisp printed-wood surface at a lower material cost, floated over a flat subfloor. Tile is the laundry-room look and the usual choice for baths, mudrooms and other wet rooms. Carpet is the soft living-room look still used in bedrooms and on stairs, with pad and transitions planned where it meets a hard floor.

When a style is close, check material ranges in the Price Guide and what else goes into a quote in the installation cost guide. We install these floors in Portland and the nearby cities on this site: Gresham, Happy Valley, Clackamas, Oregon City, Beaverton, Tigard, Lake Oswego and Vancouver, WA.
